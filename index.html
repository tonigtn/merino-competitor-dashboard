Two kinds of video

Smartwool, Icebreaker and Ridge run 8–11 second brand clips: scenery, product, logo. Woolx and Woolly run 23–29 second videos where a real person talks through a problem and a result. The second kind is the one that sells to cold traffic, and Merino Tech has neither.

Woolx’s 519 videos are mostly vertical (9:16) and fronted by creators or by “Jenny from Woolx”, a persona page the brand runs ads from.

What lasts

Brands pay to keep an ad running only while it earns. So age is the best public sign of a winner, and many copies of one idea is the second best.

    Reading it

    Woolx has 149 ads older than six months. Its most-copied idea is a customer’s own sentence used as the headline. Icebreaker has one ad older than 90 days out of 197: “Well-travelled. Still fresh.”

    Half of Merino Tech’s 30 ads are 3–6 months old. That is not a bench of winners, it is a small set left running. Its oldest, “Stay Fresh, Stay Active”, has lasted 375 days, so the odor-and-freshness promise does work.

    Where the ads send people

    A product or collection page asks a stranger to buy straight away. A dedicated page explains the problem first. Woolly sends three in four ads to a page built for that angle.

    The gap on the map

    The three heritage brands sell outdoor performance through brand storytelling. Woolx and Woolly sell everyday life through direct response: a named problem, proof, an offer. Merino Tech sits in the middle doing neither fully.

    The bottom-right corner is empty. Nobody uses problem-first, proof-heavy advertising for people who are outside in the cold because they have to be. Merino Tech’s thermal sets and heavy 250–320 GSM layers already fit that corner.

    Merino Tech’s three strongest claims, temperature, odor and comfort, are the three most crowded rows. The message is true and everyone is saying it.

    Next moves for Merino Tech

    Without a clearly superior product, the message has to be better, and a message can only be better for a specific person. Here is where to look, then what to do.

    Which person to speak toTop-left is the target: strong desire, few brands talking

    These eight groups come from gaps in competitor ads, not from customer research. Before building on either highlighted pick, run the avatar deep dive: read the Reddit threads, Amazon reviews and Facebook groups where these people talk, and confirm the desire is as strong as it looks and that they can pay.

    1. Choose one person and write only to them

      Start research on the two highlighted groups. Both have a strong daily pain, both are ignored by all six competitors, and both fit products already in the catalogue.

    2. Get video into market before November

      Zero of 30 ads are video. Brief 20–35 second creator videos where someone like the buyer names the problem in the first three seconds, the format Woolx runs 519 times.

    3. Use customers’ sentences as headlines

      Product pages already hold up to 599 reviews at 4.5–4.9 stars and Trustpilot shows 4.5. Woolx’s most-copied ad is a single review quote with the customer’s name.

    4. Build one page per angle

      Every Merino Tech ad lands on a collection or product page. Woolly’s education pages and bundle builders, and Woolx’s founder letter with cost-per-wear math, do the convincing before the price appears.

    5. Make the price the argument

      A 100% merino tee is $44.99 at Merino Tech and $85 at Woolx; an Icebreaker base layer is $120. The site has a comparison table. No ad uses it.

    6. Borrow other people’s faces

      Woolx runs ads from 9 creator, persona and publisher pages; Woolly from 2 creators and a third-party review site. An ad from a person reads as a recommendation.

    7. Test in volume, then keep the winners

      Ten new ads a month can’t find a winner. Aim for 10–15 new concepts a week through the season and leave the survivors running, as Woolx does with 149 ads past six months.
